## Rule ledger (checker enforces on copy)

- **R1** — preconditions arrive as a disqualifier with a number, never a trailing hedge.
- **R3/R11** — every substantial block contains a seller obligation ("I run that arithmetic with you," "I decline the build"), quoted from the current copy.
- **R5** — a conversion delta is a direction from a mechanism, never a promised measurement; the block says so.
- **R6** — every printed count recomputes from a rate + base printed on the same page.
- **R7** — no range whose top exceeds 3× its floor travels as one claim; split into named scenarios (Lean/Strong), defend one.
- **R9** — cut any sentence a competitor could paste unchanged (no figure, no obligation, no client-checkable fact).
- **R10** — a disqualifier prints both sides: the cost/band appears beside the ceiling.

## Presentation directions (distinct per iteration — the experiment)

- **v2 Sourced — data-forward editorial.** Serif reading body (Newsreader/Spectral), superscript footnote marks, a real Sources list at the foot, one restrained accent. Menu: slim top tabs. Objects: cited stat callouts. Register: credible, calm.
- **v3 Expanded — napkin worksheet / technical.** Mono accents (Space Mono / IBM Plex Mono), Lean vs Strong scenario **tables** front and centre, disqualifier **callout boxes**, an "obligation ledger" list. Menu: a section stepper (left rail or sticky). Register: show-the-math.
- **v4 Custom — quiet premium.** Mercury/Anthropic calm: generous whitespace, warm near-white, one refined accent, a serif or humanist sans, minimal objects, a single confident CTA. Register: less-is-more, human.

All three: bigger body (20px+), wide-enough measure, gradients on grounds not inside lined blocks, a version switcher, CTAs to `#` placeholders (Join the course → EEC landing; Reply in the DM → LinkedIn thread) with a code comment.

## v2 — Sourced copy (author draft; checker to pass)

**Headline.** Of every 1,000 visitors your construction-software site earns, 950–980 leave without a trace. Educational email brings a share of them back — taught, and using the account.

**Who.** For construction software with a trial or freemium tier — field management, bidding, scheduling, job costing — where signups arrive and most never reach a second session.

**The baseline, counted.** A SaaS landing page converts a median ~3.8% of visitors; the B2B working band is 2–5%¹, and a demo-request page runs 1.5–4%¹. Of 1,000 visitors, 20–50 take the primary door and 950–980 leave without a trace — reachable again only at ad rates. I count your real 1,000, not the benchmark, before I quote anything.

**The second door (EEC).** The visitor who declines the demo takes a free five-day course on a problem he already has — in construction software, crew adoption. Welcome-class email is the highest-opening send there is, above 40%²; Apple's Mail Privacy Protection inflates that by 15–20 points², so I read clicks with you, not opens. Completion is the qualification: whoever finishes five lessons on crew adoption has verifiably got the problem, and the first call starts inside a frame you wrote.

**Disqualifier (both sides).** At 400 visits/month the chain is small enough to name: ~1.6% become addresses (≈6/month), ~5% of those convert — about one conversion a quarter, against a mid-four-figure build. I run that arithmetic on your real sessions before a lesson is written, and if the ceiling is under the cost, I say so and we work above the funnel instead.

**Behavioral.** Trial systems with no sequenced mail convert 3–5% trial→paid⁴; sequenced mail moves the median upward — a direction from the mechanism, not a promised number (R5). The surest line is plumbing: involuntary churn — failed cards, customers who meant to stay — is 20–40% of all churn³. On 45 cancels a year that is 9–18 failed-card cancels; lifting recovery from ~30% to ~60%³ keeps an extra ~3–5 accounts, for the least writing of anything here.

**Disqualifier.** Under ~50 signups/month, a founder writing personally beats any system — I decline the build.

**Close.** Three ways on: Join the course (primary) · Read the reasoning · Reply in the DM.

Sources: the four numbered references above, printed as a list.

## v3 — Expanded copy (from ITERATIONS-source.md §4–§5; checker to pass)

Lead: **The arithmetic, on your numbers.** Every figure recomputes from a rate and a base on this page (R6); wide bands split into Lean (defended) and Strong (ceiling) scenarios (R7).

**EEC — per 10,000 sessions/month, person-based.** Sessions ÷1.4 ≈ 7,150 people; ×0.8 ≈ 5,700 buying units. Render the Lean/Strong table (from §5.1):

| Step | Lean (defended) | Strong (ceiling) |
|---|---|---|
| Unique people (÷1.4 Lean / ÷1.2 Strong) | ~7,150 | ~8,300 |
| Primary door, no course (1–3% / 4–7% of sessions) | ~100–300/mo | ~400–700/mo |
| Popup: email inserted (1.8% / 3.9% of people) | ~129 | ~324 |
| Course CTA → page → email inserted (2%→20% / 5%→40%) | ~29 | ~166 |
| New addresses/month (sum of both paths) | ~158 | ~490 |
| Reach lesson 5 (25% / 40% of addresses) | ~40 | ~196 |
| Trial or conversation (5% / 15% of addresses) | ~8/mo | ~73/mo |
| Standing on list after sequence | ~150 | ~417 |
| Addresses at month 12 (×10.7, 2%/mo unsub) | ~1,600 | ~4,460 |

Disqualifier box (R10): the instrument earns its mid-four-figure cost from ~1,400 people/mo (~2,000 sessions) up; at ~290 people (400 sessions) it yields one conversion a quarter — I decline that build. I say which side of marginal your sessions count puts you on before a single lesson gets built.

**Behavioral — per 1,000 signups, forked (R4/R7).**

| System | Trial→paid | Paid of 1,000 | ARR delta @ $3.3k ACV |
|---|---|---|---|
| No mail | 3–5% | 30–50 | — |
| Drip (calendar; ships today) | +2 pts | +20 | +$66k |
| Behavioral, less-than-optimal (2 triggers, guessed thresholds) | +1 pt over drip | +10 over drip | +$33k over drip |
| Behavioral, vendor ceiling (refused as a promise) | +5–10 pts | +50–100 | $165–330k |

The well-set gate — the first deliverable is a ten-minute map of which systems already emit a reachable event, scored on four binary checks: Defined · Routable · Identity-joined · Historied (~90 days). Fail any and behavioral is drip in costume — I sell drip, at drip's price, with the wiring named as the gap. Under ~50 signups a month I decline the build: at that volume you writing personally beats any system.

## v4 — Custom copy (my synthesis; checker to pass)

**Headline.** Your signups leave before the product gets to prove itself. I bring them back taught.

**Movement 1 — the problem.** A signup arrives at full attention and spends it fast. The product asks for more — another setup step, another tooltip — and the tab closes. Nothing on the calendar reaches the person after that; the funnel reports a rate and hides every individual exit.

**Movement 2 — the two instruments.** A five-day course teaches the problem before it asks for anything, and ends at the product — the visitor who declined the demo still learns, and comes back using the account. Behavioral email fires on the moment a customer acts — setup abandoned, trial gone quiet, card declined — instead of on a date. Calendar mail hits the week; a trigger hits the moment.

**Movement 3 — the honesty.** I run the arithmetic on your real numbers before a lesson is written. Below the floor — under ~50 signups a month, or under ~400 sessions a month — I tell you to wait, and say which side of marginal you're on. The list keeps working after a campaign stops; a demo booked once is spent once.

**Close.** One door: Join the course. (Read the reasoning · Reply in the DM as quiet secondaries.)
