## §R — Rules (live, versioned)

The principle behind all of them (carried from the source note): ban the safe move **by name** — a general instruction loses to a specific one. Each rule below is enforceable by inspection, without judgment calls.

- **R1** — A precondition arrives as a disqualifier with a number, never as a trailing hedge. "At 400 visits a month this recovers a rounding error" passes; "results depend on traffic" is banned.
- **R2 → R2′ (altered this iteration, on Andrew's direction 2026-08-04).**
  - R2 (v1): every magnitude carries its source class or gets cut, and the cut is logged.
  - R2′ (v2): a cut range **returns** inside napkin math as a bounded range with its class marked inline — `[median-benchmark]`, `[vendor]`, `[course-seller]`, `[ceiling — MPP-inflated]`, `[if]` for assumption, `⌀` for no data. Ceiling-metrics (opens) always written as "up to." What stays banned: the same figure as a bare point number inside a **spoken line**, and any figure travelling on analogy (retail → SaaS) presented as a SaaS number.
  - Why the alternation: the v1 cut deleted the arithmetic along with the overclaim. Napkin math needs the range; the reader needs the class. Marking beats deleting.
- **R3** — Each iteration's longest block must contain at least one sentence that obliges the seller to a checkable action ("I run that arithmetic with you before a single lesson gets built"). Cold-reader test: if the answer to "what does this oblige the writer to do?" is "nothing," the block fails regardless of compliance.
- **R4** — Load-bearing claims ship with their fork: both strengths written, the chosen one defended. A weak form that survives every outcome claims nothing and loses by default.
- **R5** — (from NAPKIN §6, inherited) A conversion delta is a directional expectation from a mechanism, never a promised measurement — and the block says so where a founder would read it.

Adopted from the cold inspection (§3), 2026-08-04 — all six proposals executed in §4:

- **R6 — Ban the orphan number.** Every printed count or dollar figure must recompute, at both endpoints, from a rate and a base printed on the same page. A figure needing an unstated coefficient (ARPA, attrition, lift-points, sample size) is banned until the coefficient is printed. A table row with "—" for its count leaves the table.
- **R7 — Cap the span.** No range whose top exceeds 3× its floor travels as a single claim. Wider spans split into named scenarios with each endpoint's driver printed, and one scenario is defended per R4.
- **R8 — A class-mark carries class only.** No sentence, disclaimer, or argument inside brackets; anything editorial is a spoken line and obeys spoken-line rules. A named "honest floor" gets a table row with a count, never a bracket.
- **R9 — Ban the pasteable sentence.** Any sentence in a presentation block containing no figure, no seller obligation, and no client-checkable fact is cut. The test: could a competitor paste it unchanged.
- **R10 — A disqualifier prints both sides of its comparison.** "Under the cost" is banned unless the cost or its band appears beside the ceiling.
- **R11 — The obligation register quotes.** Each "what this obliges" line must quote a sentence verbatim from the **current** iteration's block; a citation resolvable only to a superseded iteration fails.

## §1 — Iteration 1 (record; superseded by §2)

### EEC — three lines, increasing volume
1. Five emails that teach the mechanism behind your buyer's worst problem — whoever finishes all five is a qualified lead.
2. The visitor who declines your demo will still take a free five-day course on a problem he already has. Each lesson ends at a step he can take that day; lesson five ends at your product. Completion is the qualification — no form asks him to self-describe.
3. An Educational Email Course is a page plus five lessons teaching the mechanism behind a problem your market already names — in construction software, crew adoption: 47% of contractors call getting their own people onto new technology their biggest challenge. The visitor who won't book a demo takes the course; the one who finishes five lessons on crew adoption has verifiably got the problem, and your first call starts inside a frame you wrote. It needs arrivals to work on — at 400 visits a month it recovers a rounding error, and I run that arithmetic with you before a single lesson gets built.

### Behavioral — three lines, increasing volume
1. Mail that fires when a user does something — or conspicuously stops.
2. Drip fires on a calendar whether or not anyone moved. Behavioral fires on the act: setup abandoned, no login in ten days, card declined. Two of those triggers are reachable today through webhooks you already run; the rest wait on events nobody has defined. I start with the two.
3. Every triggered send needs an event the sending system can reach, so the first deliverable is a ten-minute map of which of your systems already emit one. Billing webhooks and the help desk are reachable now — that makes failed-payment recovery and support follow-up buildable this week, zero engineering. Failed payments alone are 20–40% of churn, from customers who intended to stay; recovering them is mostly retry plumbing, and I scope it as plumbing, with the copy priced as the minority of the lift it is. Everything else builds as its event gets defined: drip ships today, behavioral runs phase two on the three events your churn turns on.

### 5.0 The unit correction, verified
A session, a person, and a buying unit are three different denominators, and the benchmarks mix them. New data this iteration: SaaS runs **~1.4 sessions per user** (band 1.2–1.6) `[median-benchmark]`, and **returning visitors convert at 2–3× first-timers** `[median-benchmark]` — the second figure matters below. So the archetype's 10,000 sessions/month = **~7,150 people** (÷1.4) = **~5,700 buying units** (×0.8). Every person-act row below (opt-in, lesson, conversion) runs on people; the popup display row alone stays session-fed by its measurement. Iteration 3's counts were session-based and therefore ~30% overstated on capture — logged, superseded here.

**Two disqualifiers added after the natural-convert row, merits checked (one candidate rejected, logged):**

**(bonus) — opens with the window, then the floor case.**

*The considered-purchase window, as a pro — the "how so" extended.* This buyer decides in a window, never in a visit: the vault's own buying language runs "we demoed six other options," "ten or fifteen programs at least," "four years of searching" `[vault: LANGUAGE §7]`. Three mechanisms follow, in sequence:
1. **Return is the converting act, and capture removes its luck.** Returning visitors convert at 2–3× first-timers `[median-benchmark]` — the decision literally happens on the return. An anonymous researcher must re-find you past six competitors, N separate times; a captured address gets each return *delivered*, on days you chose. The EEC converts a multi-visit journey from N chances to lose the buyer into one chance to keep him.
2. **Five lessons occupy the exact days the comparison runs.** A 5–7 day sequence sits inside the shortlist-building week; by lesson five the buyer evaluates your six competitors with the criteria you taught. The frame travels into demos you're absent from.
3. **Partially for Behavioral too:** the window persists past signup — the trial and the 90-day cliff `[vault: T1 — 44% of first-year cancels]` are windows with decision *moments* inside them (stalled, hit the limit, card failed). Calendar mail hits the week; a triggered send is the only channel that can hit the moment. The window logic licenses behavioral's timing claim while its magnitude claims stay under §4.2's floors.

## §6 — Iteration 5: where the visitors come from, and what each one costs (new section, researched 2026-08-04)

The chains above run on "10,000 sessions" as a given. This section prices the given — because the same 158 addresses cost wildly different money depending on which channel delivered the sessions, and the difference decides where the EEC is rational.

### 6.1 The channel price list

| Channel | Price | Unit | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic search / SEO (content maintained) | ~$164 | per lead, blended content cost | `[median-benchmark 2025]` |
| Review badges / directories organic (G2-class) | ~$0 marginal; converts 33% vs 25% for paid-sourced | per click | `[vault: PRACTICES]` |
| Google Search Ads, SaaS keywords | ~$8.86–15.36 · CPL ~$70 | per click / per lead | `[median-benchmark — B2B tech vs pure-SaaS]` |
| LinkedIn Ads | ~$8 median ($5–15) · CPL ~$110 | per click / per lead | `[median-benchmark]` |
| Software directories PPC (Capterra class) | $2–20 | per click | `[vault: TRAFFIC-SYSTEM]` |
| Social retargeting | $2–5 | per click | `[vault]` |
| Paid search CAC, B2B, full chain | ~$802 | per customer | `[median-benchmark 2025]` |

### 6.2 The arithmetic the price list forces

**Cost per collected address, by channel.** Lean captures ~158 addresses per 10,000 sessions ≈ **1.6% per session**. On bought clicks at $8.86–15.36, an address costs **$8.86 ÷ 0.016 ≈ $550–960**. On organic sessions the marginal cost is ~$0. That is the section's finding, stated once and load-bearing: **the EEC is a second harvest on traffic already earned; it is never a reason to buy traffic.** Buying clicks *for* the course loses ~7–12× against the standard paid path (a dedicated ad landing page converting ~10%+ to a form, CPL $70–110) — where paid runs, it runs to the demo page, and the course catches its decliners as a by-product.

**What this does to the disqualifiers.** "The door before the net" (§5.1) gets its price tag: a vendor buying $802-CAC paid search through a sub-1% door burns ~$9–15 per leaked session *twice* — once losing the demo, once losing the address. And the ~2,000-session floor is really an *organic* floor: 2,000 bought sessions cost ~$18–30k/month, at which spend the mid-four-figure course build is noise in the wrong direction.

**Where Behavioral sits, channel-wise.** Channel-independent by construction — it runs on signups, whoever delivered them. The channel data sharpens its value instead: at ~$200–700 SMB CAC (paid-search chain ~$802) `[median-benchmark]`, every signup already carries sunk acquisition cost, so the +2pts drip delta and the dunning saves are recoveries *of spend already made* — retention mail is the only channel row whose denominator was already paid for.

### 6.3 What Iteration 5 obliges (R11)
"Where paid runs, it runs to the demo page" — I never propose ad spend routed at the course; a channel-mix read (paid share, per-channel door rate) joins the day-one analytics check, priced inside the existing scope.
